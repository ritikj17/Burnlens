# Metrics

The North Star metric is **qualified savings dollars captured per week**. A generic visitor or daily active user metric is wrong for this product because founders may only audit AI spend quarterly. The product exists to create credible savings opportunities and route the best ones to Credex. A completed audit with $0 savings is still trust-building, but the business value comes from high-confidence, high-savings reports.

Three input metrics drive the North Star:

1. **Audit completion rate** — started audits that reach a result page. If this is low, the form is too long or confusing.
2. **High-savings lead rate** — completed audits with more than $500/month savings that submit email. This measures whether the tool is reaching the right audience.
3. **Consultation click/book rate** — high-savings reports that click or book a Credex consult. This measures whether the report is persuasive enough to create action.

First instrumentation should track: landing page view, audit started, tool row enabled, audit completed, report shared, lead captured, consultation clicked, and email sent. I would also log savings band, team-size band, primary use case, and tools included, but not email or company identity in analytics.

A pivot trigger: after 500 completed audits from relevant startup channels, if fewer than 3% show savings above $500/month or fewer than 20% of high-savings reports capture email, the positioning is off. Either the tool is attracting hobbyists with tiny spend, or the recommendations are not trusted enough to exchange contact information.
