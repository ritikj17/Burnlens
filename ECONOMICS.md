# Economics

The value of a converted lead for Credex mainly depends on how much discounted AI infrastructure or credits a startup ends up purchasing and whether they continue buying over time.

A reasonable starting assumption is that a qualified startup purchases around $25,000 worth of AI credits or discounted infrastructure over its first year. If Credex operates at roughly a 12% margin, that would generate about $3,000 in gross profit from the initial purchase.

There is also a reasonable chance that some customers expand usage later. If around 35% of customers purchase an additional $15,000 over time at the same margin, that adds roughly another $630 in expected gross profit.

That puts the estimated value of one converted customer at approximately **$3,630 in gross profit**.

Example funnel assumptions:

- 1,000 visitors
- 350 audit starts
- 220 completed audits
- 70 saved reports
- 25 higher-intent leads
- 8 consultation calls booked
- 2 completed purchases

Using those assumptions:

- Audit completed → consultation booked = 8 / 220 = 3.6%
- Consultation booked → purchase = 2 / 8 = 25%
- Audit completed → purchase = 0.9%

At roughly $3,630 gross profit per converted customer, 220 completed audits would generate around $7,260 in expected gross profit, or about **$33 expected value per completed audit**.

The customer acquisition cost for early distribution channels is mostly time rather than paid advertising.

- X/Twitter founder replies: around 4 hours for 20 manual audits and ~5 qualified leads. Assuming a $50/hour opportunity cost, CAC is about $40 per qualified lead.
- Community posts and discussions: around 3 hours of writing and replies for ~10 qualified leads. Estimated CAC is roughly $15 per qualified lead.
- Startup newsletter partnerships: around 6 hours coordinating and preparing examples for ~30 qualified leads. Estimated CAC is roughly $10-15 per lead plus relationship overhead.
- Product Hunt or Hacker News launch: around 12 hours of prep work with highly variable results, but potentially ~40 qualified leads if the launch performs well.

The economics work because the product does not require massive conversion rates to become useful. If a completed audit is worth around $33 in expected gross profit and infrastructure costs remain low, there is still room for manual outreach and sales follow-up without needing heavy automation immediately.

To contribute roughly $1M in annualized gross profit within 18 months, Credex would need approximately:

- $1,000,000 / $3,630 ≈ 276 converted customers

At a 0.9% audit-to-purchase conversion rate, that would require roughly 30,000 completed audits over 18 months, or around 1,700 completed audits per month.

That level of volume is probably only realistic if BurnLens becomes more than a one-time calculator. The product would likely need:
- searchable pricing and benchmark content
- shareable reports
- recurring founder/operator traffic
- distribution through startup communities and newsletters
- and a growing database of anonymized spending benchmarks that make the audit more useful over time
